## Add a date

Type `@` at the start of a line, or after a space. An `@` right after other characters, as in an email address, opens nothing.

A list opens with the date your words make, if they make one. **Today**, **Tomorrow**, **Yesterday** and **Now** come after it, narrowing as you type, and [notes](#link-to-another-note) come last.

- **On the Mac**, use ↑ and ↓ to move through the list, and press Return or click to pick. Esc closes the list and leaves what you typed.
- **On the iPhone**, tap a row. The **@** button in the bar above the keyboard types the `@` for you, with a space first if the caret is right after a word.

### What you can type after `@`

- **A name:** `today`, `tomorrow`, `yesterday` or `now`
- **A weekday:** `friday` or `fri`, for the next one. Today's own weekday means a week from today.
- **A month and day:** `aug 5` or `august 5`, this year
- **Numbers, month first:** `8/5` for this year, or `8/5/2026`
- **Year first:** `2026-08-05`
- **A time:** `3pm`, `3:30pm` or `15:30`, for today at that time
- **A date and a time:** `aug 5 3:30pm` or `friday 3pm`

While your words don't make a date yet, the Mac says **Type a date or a note's name**, unless a note matches. The iPhone shows no list until something matches.

### What a date shows

A date mention holds a fixed day, or a fixed moment if it has a time. Picking **Today** stores today's date. What it says then depends on the day you read it:

- **Today**, **Tomorrow** or **Yesterday**
- **The weekday's name**, for up to six days ahead
- **Last** and the weekday's name, for up to six days back
- **The date itself**, such as Nov 5, 2026, further away

Written on a Tuesday, it says **Today** that day, **Yesterday** on Wednesday and **Last Tuesday** by Thursday. A date with a time shows the time after it. **Now** stores the moment you picked it, with the time.

A date mention doesn't make a task or a reminder, and it doesn't show on the calendar.

## Link to another note

Type `[[` to list notes only, or `@` to see them under the dates. `[[` opens in the same places `@` does.

The list shows up to five notes, the most recently edited first. Typing narrows it: titles that start with your words come first, then titles that contain them. Case and accents don't matter. The note you are in isn't offered. On the Mac, a `[[` with no matches says **No notes match**.

Pick one the way you pick a date. The link shows the other note's title in grey, with a small book before it.

The link shows the title as it is when the note holding it opens. After you rename a note, links to it show the new title the next time their note opens.

## Open a link to a note

### On the Mac

A click shows a card with the note's title, **Open** and **Remove**. **⌘-click** opens the note straight away. **⌘K** with the caret on the link shows the card too.

After you follow a link, **Back** appears at the start of the toolbar and steps back through the links you followed. Picking a note in the list clears it.

### On the iPhone

A tap shows **Open** and **Remove**. **Open** takes you to the note on the Notes tab, from any page. The **Link** button in the **Format** bar shows the same menu when the caret is on the link.

### When the note is gone

If the other note has been deleted, the link keeps the words it had. The card or menu says **Couldn't load** and offers **Remove**. **Remove** turns any link to a note into ordinary words.

## How a mention behaves

- **The caret can't stop inside one.** One press of Backspace removes it whole.
- **Return keeps it whole.** It stays on one side of the split.
- **Formatting passes it by.** [Bold, italic and the rest](/features/formatting) don't change a mention that sits among the words you format.
- **Copying keeps it inside Matter.** Pasted into another note, it is still a mention. Other apps get its words: the date as it reads now, or the note's title.
- **Find in a note skips it.** [Find](/features/folders-and-search#find-in-a-note) doesn't look inside mentions. The search over the note list does read them, as they read when the note was last saved.

## In a title

On the Mac a note's title takes `@` for dates, with no notes in its list and no `[[`. On the iPhone a title is plain text, so renaming a note there drops any mention in its title.

Mentions work in [the Journal](/features/journal) and [the Pin board](/features/pin-board) as they do in a note.
